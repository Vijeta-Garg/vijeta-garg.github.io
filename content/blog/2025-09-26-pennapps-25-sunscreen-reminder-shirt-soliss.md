I didn't win an award this time, but built a super cool FUNCTIONAL WEARABLE : D (I applied skills from BWSI!)&nbsp;</p><h4>1) What is Soliss?</h4><div>Over my 36 h - 7h = 29h journey, I built Soliss with a team I found at the hackathon. This was a wearable that could track excess UV radiation hitting the skin and remind the wearer to reapply sunscreen. The wearable also connected to an app which allowed the user to track overall danger levels of UV radiation on their skin (calculated via a formal algorithm, and personalized to the user's demographic etc). Personally, I created the wearable part of the project (so all the hardware etc). In my 29 hours I prototyped/designed, breadboarded, coded, and the hardest part of all (that I should have left more time to)...took apart my precious breadboard and integrated the circuitry with the better: you couldn't...wear the wearable exactly. I didn't have enough time to sew in all the components, and I realized afterwards that my microcontroller had no way of being stitched into the fabric (big mistake)...next time choose components more wisely. I had to incorporate the breadboard ON the shirt : O This is an easy fix with the right microcontroller + access to soldering though&nbsp;</div><div>*I could have come up with a more elaborate idea/way to do this/could have brought supplies beforehand. I found NOW that a lot of people come to hackathons with ideas preplanned etc (some super cool person I met won edtech category and had their idea planned out in JUNE) so this is something I need to bring to my next hackathon</div><div>*Great: I gained so much confidence in my hardware skills. I had no idea I would build this and built a mock wearable in just one weekend (granted I didn't sleep but). Part of the reason I didn't preplan/bring hardware supplies was because I wasn't confident if I would get the chance to do hardware/could do it right. But now I know I can (and that I love to do it), so I can fearlessly bring all my materials on the next one&nbsp;</div><div>*Great: limited availability of materials meant I got creative with how to use them...including making a TEXTILE SENSOR out of GUM WRAPPERS : D&nbsp;</div><div><br /></div><div>Overall, I learned so much from this hackathon. It was more competitive than HackMIT BP, I would say. A lot of people were from unis + there for venture funding/internships as well. I understood how hackathons work a bit better now (you should brainstorm beforehand!), gained confidence in my skills, and am happy with the project I walked out with.
